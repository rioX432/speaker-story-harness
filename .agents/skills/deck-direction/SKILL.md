# Direction and rhythm, not a competing story

Adapted from `deck-direction`. After the story is approved, decide which moments benefit from contrast, demonstration, surprise or recalling the opening example. Use expectation and reversal only when supported by data; do not fabricate a heroic failure narrative.

A 12-minute LT has little room for filler, ornamental title slides or imposed jokes. An important observation deserves room and a beat of silence. Retain visual vocabulary across comparisons; change the relevant dimension, not the whole composition, when showing stepwise transformations.

Reread the entire deck in speaker sequence and ask: Does each change of scene feel earned? Is there an unexplained acronym or abrupt topic switch? Can the final decision be recognized as the answer to the opening question?
